# bulleye

A free ebook reader for Windows and Linux with an intuitive and easy to use interface. It opens EPUB (Electronic Publication), MOBI (Mobipocket) and AZW3 (Amazon Kindle Format 8) files.

## Download

- **Windows:** download `bulleye-setup.exe` from the [Releases page](https://github.com/drawerjo/bulleye/releases), double-click it, and bulleye installs and opens. No administrator rights needed.
- **Linux:** download `bulleye-linux-x64.tar.gz` from the Releases page, extract it, and run the file named `bulleye`.

bulleye updates itself automatically when you are online.

On Windows, a blue "Windows protected your PC" message may appear because the app is new. Press **More info**, then **Run anyway**.

## What it does

- Scroll through a book continuously, like a PDF reader, with a fixed page count that does not change when you zoom.
- One-page or two-page view, zoom, and page-by-page turning with the arrow keys.
- A library with covers, progress, last-opened dates and sorting. Add a whole folder at once.
- Bookmarks, and it remembers where you stopped in every book.
- Highlights in four colours and notes attached to any passage.
- Search inside a book, with every match listed by chapter.
- Read aloud using the voices already on your computer, with speed control.
- A dictionary: select a word to look it up (needs internet).
- Auto scroll with adjustable speed.
- Dark, Light, Sepia and Black themes, plus your choice of accent colour.
- Copy text to paste into notes or a browser.
- More features to be added.

## What it does not do

- It cannot open books protected by DRM (Digital Rights Management) yet.
- There is no macOS version.

## Privacy

Your books, notes, bookmarks and reading positions stay on your computer. The dictionary sends the word you select to a public dictionary service (dictionaryapi.dev or Wiktionary). bulleye also sends an anonymous daily count to help the maker understand how many people use it. It contains no personal information and nothing about your books.
